## Notes per question

**Q1. Recipes from the three papers.**
- 2609.15706 is the only one of the three with a full recipe: LoRA r16/α32 on attention + MLP, 3–5 epochs, 6–14 A100-hours for 3B/7B, about 3K synthetic samples, no learning curve.
- Its "F1>0.98" holds only in-distribution on template-rendered checks. Out of distribution, the fine-tuned 3B drops to 0.593, below the smaller fully fine-tuned SmolVLM2-2.2B at 0.788. This is the clearest evidence that templated-synthetic fine-tuning can overfit layouts, which matters for heterogeneous real EN/ES/DE/RO medical documents.
- RAPTOR+ trained on only 176 synthetic forms, and its recipe was not visible in the HTML.
- The useful RAPTOR+ signal: fine-tuning lifts 2B reading accuracy by about 26 points, but strict safety stays at about 21% at 2B and 26% at 4B against 61% at 8B.
- The VAREX "+81 points" belongs to NuMind's NuExtract 2.0, not an IBM fine-tune. VAREX also shows that a large part of small-model failure is format and schema-following, partly fixable by prompt and schema design (inlining `$defs`) before any training.

**Q2. LoRA together with mmproj in llama.cpp.**
- Both flags exist on `llama-mtmd-cli` and `llama-server`.
- No retrieved source confirms a runtime text-LoRA + mmproj setup working end to end, and none reports it failing.
- Unsloth's default vision LoRA also trains the vision layers. Those weights live in the mmproj and would not be carried by a text-decoder GGUF adapter. To use a runtime adapter, train with the vision toggle off (`finetune_vision_layers=False`).
- Otherwise, merge the adapter into the base, then re-export both the LLM GGUF and the mmproj.
- Unsloth's current docs do not document mmproj export for VLMs. The only history found (PR #1904, issue #2290) shows early problems, status unknown.
- I could not evidence a successful Qwen3-VL or Gemma 3 fine-tune → GGUF → llama.cpp chain this round.
- The 2025 "@" output report from round 1 was not re-checked.

**Q3. Cost.**
- Hard numbers: 6–14 A100-80GB GPU-hours for LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B with about 3K samples over 3–5 epochs (2609.15706).
- VRAM: Qwen3.5-2B needs about 5GB and Qwen3.5-4B about 10GB for bf16 LoRA (Unsloth), so a T4, L4 or consumer GPU fits.
- No published wall-clock figure for MedGemma 4B LoRA was found. Papers found say "Colab A100" but give no runtime.
- Unsloth says not to use QLoRA for Qwen3.5. That removes the cheapest 4-bit path but has little effect on cost at 2–4B.

**Q4. Licences.**
- Qwen3.5-2B: Apache-2.0, card checked. Gemma 4: Apache-2.0, official blog via search, LICENSE file not opened. Both allow shipping a fine-tuned derivative in a commercial app with standard Apache notice obligations.
- MedGemma and its derivatives carry HAI-DEF obligations: flow-down of use restrictions into the app's ToS or EULA, a copy of the Agreement to recipients, a Notice file, health regulatory authorisation "when applicable", and a ban on uses that could make Google a device "manufacturer".
- Whether MedGemma 1.5 is under the same terms was not checked.

**Q5. Data.**
- There is no maintained European Synthea fork. synthea-international plus profile-conversion work (German MII) is the state of play.
- No FHIR-to-PDF or document-rendering tool was found this round; the budget ran out.
- GRPO with schema-validity rewards exists as patterns: open-r1 PR #722, the VLM-R1 format reward, the TRL VLM GRPO cookbook, and Unsloth's Qwen3.5-4B GRPO notebook.
- No paper was found showing GRPO schema rewards on 2–4B VLMs for document → JSON with numbers.
- Distillation from large-model labels was not searched. Note that HAI-DEF counts distillation outputs used to train another model as Model Derivatives (§1.1(h)), so MedGemma-labelled data used to train a Qwen model would inherit HAI-DEF terms.
