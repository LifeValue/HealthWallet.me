## Notes per question

**Q1 (releases May→Oct 2026).** Most hypotheses were discarded. Qwen has released nothing of 4B or less since May (#2). Its current small line is Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B/4B from March 2026 (#1), and it is newer than the Qwen3-VL-2B in the app. Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 are 27B and up. I found no "Qwen3-VL-4B refresh" or "Qwen4-VL small". Gemma 4 (April 2026, #20) brings E2B/E4B on-device variants with an official LiteRT-LM path (#15, #19). Gemma 3n has effectively been superseded (inference; I did not check Gemma 3n directly). MedGemma 1.5 4B (January 2026, #10) is the latest MedGemma I found, and I saw no Gemma 4-based MedGemma (#14). New releases inside the window: PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (2026-05-28, #21), TeleOCR (August paper, no weights confirmed, #24), and possibly LFM2.5-VL-3B (August, unconfirmed, #30). LFM2.5-VL-1.6B and 450M exist, but I have no exact release date (#27). Phi's successor is 15B, so it is out (#31). The budget did not stretch to InternVL3.5/4, MiniCPM-V 4.x/5, SmolVLM/Granite-Docling, dots.ocr, Nanonets-OCR, olmOCR, DeepSeek-OCR (there is a DeepSeek-OCR token-pruning paper, arXiv 2605.00392, which suggests it is still active) or Moondream. Those are open.

**Q2 (specs and benchmarks against the incumbents).** The clearest like-for-like comparison is Qwen3.5-2B against Qwen3-VL-2B on Qwen's own card: +6 OCRBench, +15 OmniDocBench1.5, +7.5 CC-OCR, plus medical VQA gains (#3–#5). These are vendor numbers. Gemma 4 reports OmniDocBench1.5 as edit distance (#16) while Qwen reports an overall score (#4), so the two cannot be compared directly. MedGemma 1.5 against MedGemma 1 is the most decision-relevant medical result: lab-report PDF→JSON F1 goes from 25 to 64 (#11). It is vendor-run on mostly internal datasets, and some VQA scores fell (#12). LFM2.5-VL-1.6B reports OCRBench **v2**, a harder and different benchmark from Qwen's OCRBench, so its 41.44 is not comparable with Qwen's 85.4 (#27). PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 leads OmniDocBench v1.6 (#22), but it is a parser (text, tables, layout) rather than an instruction-following extractor, and OmniDocBench is reportedly saturated (#26). Size and RAM gates: Qwen3.5-2B is in the same class as Qwen3-VL-2B. Gemma 4 E2B carries 5.1B total parameters, and E4B carries 8B, because of Per-Layer Embeddings (#15). Their on-disk and RAM footprint in LiteRT form is **unverified**, and E4B may break the ~3.3 GB advanced tier, which needs checking. Runtime compatibility risk: Qwen3.5 is a Gated-DeltaNet hybrid (#7). Upstream llama.cpp supports it (#8), but whether the app's bundled llama.cpp build does is unverified.

**Q3 (independent evals on medical document extraction).** I found none for small on-device VLMs. The only structured lab-report extraction numbers are Google's own MedGemma figures (#11). The nearest independent clinical work is server-side 35B (#32). ConfBench (arXiv 2608.01792) and ExtractBench were named in a search summary as document-extraction benchmarks, and I did not read them.

**Q4 (multilingual OCR for RO/DE/ES).** I found no Romanian document-OCR benchmark (#33, #34). On language coverage alone: Qwen3.5 claims 201 languages (#6), Gemma 4 claims 35+ supported (#18), and LFM2.5-VL lists German and Spanish but not Romanian (#28). The MedGemma 1.5 card makes no multilingual claim and was evaluated mainly in English (#13). PaddleOCR-VL-1.6's card does not list its languages (#23). None of this is evidence of OCR accuracy on Romanian diacritics, so the team should test it themselves.

### Shortlist
1. **Qwen3.5-2B (GGUF): likely Lite-tier replacement for Qwen3-VL-2B.** It beats the incumbent on every document and medical benchmark reported on the same card (#3–#5), is Apache-2.0, claims the most languages, and has community GGUFs (#9). Risks: the hybrid architecture needs runtime support (#7, #8), the thinking mode can loop (#6), and it predates the May window.
2. **MedGemma 1.5 4B: drop-in upgrade for the advanced tier's MedGemma 1 4B.** It has the same size class and large vendor-reported gains in lab-report and EHR PDF→JSON extraction (#11). Risks: HAI-DEF terms (presumably the same as the MedGemma already shipped, but in-app redistribution wording is unverified here), English-centric evaluation (#13), and some VQA regressions (#12).
3. **Qwen3.5-4B: advanced-tier challenger.** Same licence and family as #1. I did not retrieve its own scores this run.
4. **Gemma 4 E2B/E4B (LiteRT-LM): runtime-diverse alternative.** Apache 2.0, an official Google on-device stack with MTP speedup (#19), a tunable image-token budget that matters for latency (#17), and OmniDocBench and MedXPertQA scores reported (#16). Risks: the 5.1B/8B total parameters against the memory gates (unverified), and no DocVQA or OCRBench figures to compare with Qwen.
5. **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B): an OCR/layout front-end stage, not an end-to-end extractor.** Apache-2.0, released inside the window, and the strongest reported scores on parsing and real-capture robustness (#21, #22). It would feed a text LLM or VLM. Mobile runtime and language coverage are unverified (#23).
6. *(Watch)* **LFM2.5-VL-1.6B**: an efficient tiling scheme and GGUF/ONNX/MLX builds (#29), but no Romanian (#28), no head-to-head with Qwen, and lfm1.0 commercial terms I did not verify.

### Cuts
- Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B, Gemma 4 12B/26B/31B, Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8: over the parameter gate (#2, #20, #31).
- TeleOCR: no size, weights or weights licence confirmed (#24).
- MinerU2.5-Pro and GLM-OCR: only a low-confidence secondary ranking; their size and licence were not checked (#25).
- LFM2.5-VL-3B: existence not confirmed from a primary source (#30).
- The rest of the hypothesis list was not evaluated (budget), so those models are not cuts on the merits.

- arXiv 2606.03264 (PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 report): language list and per-scenario Real5 numbers.
- ConfBench (arXiv 2608.01792) and ExtractBench: independent document-extraction and JSON benchmarks that may include small VLMs.
