## Notes per question

**Q1 Metrics.** Published practice in 2026 converges on these parts:
- Deterministic matching of predicted to gold records with the Hungarian algorithm (ExtractBench/LlamaIndex, VAREX, ANLS*).
- Flattened field-level P/R/F1, where extra records hurt precision and missing records hurt recall (#1, #2).
- A per-field comparator declared in the schema: exact for codes and IDs, numeric tolerance for quantities, fuzzy for names, LLM-semantic only for free text (#6).
- Explicit present/null/MISSING states, which give clean omission and hallucination rates (#7).
- Invalid output kept in the denominator, with validity reported separately (#8, #9).
- Grounding to page or bounding box, an emerging metric (#3).

For FHIR specifically:
- Validity is measured with the official HL7 validator (error-severity only).
- Code accuracy is exact code match (TGA).
- Completeness is checked against required profile fields (#18).
- No retrieved FHIR paper defines resource-level bipartite matching plus field F1. That gap is the design space this benchmark has to fill.

Not retrieved this run, so unverified beliefs: TEDS and nTED definitions, UCUM-aware normalisation, partial credit for display text versus code, and calibration or abstention metrics. ANLS*'s penalty for answering an unanswerable question is the closest retrieved analogue to abstention scoring.

**Q2 Datasets.**
- No public document-image→FHIR benchmark was found.
- Text→FHIR work: FHIR Workbench, FHIR-GPT (MedicationStatement only), the schema-grounded paper (MIMIC-IV with n2c2, credentialed access, partly placeholder numbers) and CIRCA (intents, credentialed MIMIC layers).
- FHIR-AgentBench is QA, not extraction.
- Usable licensed generic KIE references since 2026: VAREX (CC BY 4.0, synthetic forms, with text and image modalities, and the small-model findings matter here), Contextual AI's ExtractBench (MIT) and LlamaIndex's ExtractBench (licence not seen).
- None is multilingual medical. PulseBench-Tab is multilingual tables (low confidence).
- CORD, SROIE, DocILE, OmniDocBench and KIE-Bench were not checked this run.

**Q3 Harness.**
- flexpa/llm-fhir-eval shows promptfoo with custom FHIRPath and validator assertions working for FHIR (#24).
- ExtractBench (LlamaIndex) shows a deterministic custom scorer (#1).
- On-device measurement references: MobileAIBench (iOS app) and the S25 Ultra llama.cpp study (TTFT, energy per token).
- The schema-grounded paper fixed temperature at 0.0.
- Not verified this run: lmms-eval, VLMEvalKit, Inspect, DeepEval, and seed or quantisation pinning practice.

**Q4 LLM-as-judge.**
- The evidence argues for deterministic scoring wherever the field is factual or coded.
- The 2026 healthcare scoping review shows weak validation practice and a judge monoculture (#28, #29).
- Judge accuracy collapses on non-standard formats (#30, low).
- Agreement holds for factual fields but not interpretive ones (#31, low).
- Contextual AI's ExtractBench uses a judge for array alignment, which makes scores non-deterministic. LlamaIndex's ExtractBench deliberately avoids one.
- Reasonable design: keep a judge only for free-text fields (Condition notes, dosage text), and validate it against humans on a sample.

**Q5 Gold set.**
- Synthetic, deterministic ground truth (VAREX "Reverse Annotation", filling templates with synthetic values) fits a setting where real patient documents are sensitive. Its limit is realism: no real scan degradation, as far as retrieved.
- Annotation by model consensus plus human review of disagreements is precedented (CIRCA, 88.4% agreement with humans).
- For sizing, Card et al. is the standard power reference. Paired bootstrap and NLPStatTest exist as tools (snippet).
- No retrieved source gives a document count for detecting a given F1 difference in extraction. Retrieved IAA example: entity-level κ 0.81 on a 20-note double-reviewed subset (#18 source).

## Leads worth chasing
- Full text of the LlamaIndex ExtractBench (2607.29677, offset 100000) for the licence, repo, and the exact flattening and Hungarian cost function.
- VAREX full paper for null handling and ANLS settings; its small-model and fine-tuning numbers bear directly on LoRA planning.
- Infherno repo (github.com/j-frei/Infherno) for how it scores FHIR against gold, and its licence.
- FHIR Workbench JMIR full text: how note-to-FHIR F1 is computed.
- flexpa/llm-fhir-eval assertion code and LICENSE.
- Medical document KIE or VQA datasets (lab reports, prescriptions), especially Spanish, German and Romanian.
- TEDS/nTED, and calibration or abstention metrics for extraction (selective prediction, coverage-risk curves).
- lmms-eval and VLMEvalKit support for custom JSON-extraction tasks; llama.cpp `llama-bench` for device matrices.
- Paired bootstrap and sample-size guidance specific to F1 over documents (clustered resampling by document).
