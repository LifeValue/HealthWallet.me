Decision this research serves: whether to swap or add an on-device vision-language model, and possibly the runtime; whether to invest in LoRA on our own scan→FHIR pairs; how to harden structured JSON output; and how to benchmark mapping accuracy.

Requirements frame (from the codebase, not from research): - Hard gates: - Fully on-device and offline. - Usable from Flutter on iOS, Android, macOS and Windows. - Commercial licence. - About 4B parameters or fewer. - Fits the Lite tier (about 1.5 GB total, phones with <6 GB RAM) or the advanced tier (about 3.3 GB, ≥6 GB RAM). - Weighted preferences: - Document extraction accuracy. - Medical coding knowledge. - JSON compliance. - EN/ES/DE/RO coverage. - Size and latency. - Runtime readiness, that is, credit for not migrating. - Licence burden. - Baseline: llamadart 0.6.8 (llama.cpp) with Qwen3-VL-2B and MedGemma 4B, using a generic JSON-array GBNF grammar.

Conventions: confidence is per claim and marked where below high. "Vendor" means the model's publisher measured it.

Executive summary

What to do: 1. Upgrade the runtime we already use, not switch it. 2. Make the JSON output schema-specific. 3. Build our own benchmark before changing models or training a LoRA. 4. Benchmark NuExtract3 against MedGemma for the advanced tier. Keep Qwen3-VL-2B on the Lite tier and benchmark Qwen3.5-2B and NuExtract 2.0-2B against it. 5. Defer LoRA until the benchmark exists, and prove a LoRA works alongside the vision projector first.

The findings that drive this:

  1. The runtime moved under us, inside the same package. llamadart went from our 0.6.8 to 0.15.6 [1]. It now drives llama.cpp and Google's LiteRT-LM behind one Dart API, with JSON-schema output that raises an error on unsupported schemas instead of silently ignoring them, and runtime LoRA loading [2][3].
    • LiteRT-LM may be faster on phone GPUs: 61 vs 39 tok/s in one independent text-only test, which our verifier could not corroborate (single source, unverified) [4].
    • No official Qwen or MedGemma vision builds were found for it (medium confidence), and it has no constrained JSON through llamadart yet [5].
    • Apple's and Android's built-in models cover only some platforms. The Android one is also beta and capped at 4,000 tokens [6].
    • Upgrade llamadart, keep llama.cpp as the default backend, and evaluate LiteRT-LM as a second one.
  2. Small models fail mostly on format, and the cheap fixes come first.
    • Below 4B, VAREX found models returning the schema instead of values. Inlining $defs alone lifted Qwen3-VL-2B from 27% to 92% on affected documents [7].
    • Hard schema constraints at ≤3B guarantee validity but can cost accuracy; "reason free, constrain late" (let the model reason in free text, then constrain only the final answer) recovers most of the lost accuracy [8]. An independent benchmark confirms the cost [9], and a second independent study agrees on the fix (D3).
    • Use a flat intermediate schema with required fields first, constrain the final pass, then validate FHIR on the device [10].
  3. Gains from model upgrades are smaller than vendor cards suggest.
    • Qwen3.5-2B's claimed ~15-point document lead over our Qwen3-VL-2B shrinks to a tie on an independent benchmark [11].
    • MedGemma 1.5's extraction gains are on internal PDF sets; on the public image set it equals MedGemma 1 [12].
    • The one candidate with an extraction-specific edge is NuExtract3, a Qwen3.5-4B fine-tune under Apache-2.0. It produces about one-eighth of its base model's failed outputs (27 vs 229), though only on its vendor's own benchmark [13].
    • Benchmark NuExtract3 on the advanced tier, and don't swap the Lite model yet.
  4. LoRA is cheap to train and risky to ship.
    • Measured gains are large: 2B reading accuracy went from 62% to 88% on 176 forms [14]. Compute is tens of GPU-hours at most [15].
    • Models trained on templated synthetic data collapse on unseen layouts: F1 fell from 0.98 to 0.59 [15].
    • Three targeted searches found no report of a LoRA working alongside a vision projector in llama.cpp; llama.cpp's llama-mtmd-cli merely accepts both flags [16].
    • Apple's adapter path is obsolete on iOS and macOS 27 [17].
    • Defer LoRA until the benchmark exists, and run the LoRA + projector experiment first.

Biggest caveat: no public benchmark pairs medical document images with FHIR, in any language [18]. Almost every model number here is vendor-reported. So the first deliverable is our own EN/ES/DE/RO benchmark (D4). Every other decision in this report is provisional until it exists. 7 of 23 load-bearing claims are already past their freshness bar (see the Staleness map).

D1a. Model candidates: swap or add?

Bottom line. - Advanced tier (≥6 GB RAM): there is a real candidate to benchmark against MedGemma. NuExtract3 is NuMind's document-extraction fine-tune of Qwen3.5-4B, released May 2026 under Apache-2.0. It is the same size class as MedGemma 4B, uses an architecture llama.cpp already supports, and carries no obligations under Google's Health AI Developer Foundations (HAI-DEF) terms. - Lite tier: the evidence does not justify swapping Qwen3-VL-2B yet. Its successor, Qwen3.5-2B, ties with it on the only independent document benchmark and is about 0.45 GB larger. - Every document score below is vendor-reported unless marked otherwise. The matrix is a prior for D4's benchmark, not a substitute for it.

What changed since May 2026. - Qwen: nothing ≤4B. The current small line is still Qwen3.5 0.8B–9B from March 2026 [19]. - Google: MedGemma 1.5 4B (January 2026) is the latest MedGemma, and no Gemma 4-based MedGemma exists [12]. Gemma 4 E2B/E4B arrived in April under Apache-2.0 [20]. - Released inside the window: - NuExtract3 (about May 2026) [13]. - MiniCPM-V 4.6 (about 1B, 2026-05-11) [21]. - PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B, 2026-05-28), a page parser that turns images into text and tables rather than extracting fields [22]. - A wave of OCR-only models supported in llama.cpp [23]. These would need a second pass to produce FHIR.

D1b. On-device runtime: is llama.cpp/llamadart still the way?

Bottom line: yes, for now, but not the version we ship. The landscape did shift. Google's LiteRT-LM is now a credible cross-platform runtime with vision, and it may be faster than llama.cpp on phone GPUs (one independent text benchmark, not corroborated). Apple added image input to its on-device model.

None of the alternatives beats llama.cpp on the combination we need: - vision for our models (Qwen, MedGemma); - grammar-constrained JSON; - runtime LoRA; - Windows support; - a production Flutter path.

And llamadart itself now wraps LiteRT-LM. Upgrade llamadart from 0.6.8 to 0.15.x, and evaluate LiteRT-LM as a second backend in the same package, rather than switching runtimes.

D2. Fine-tuning and LoRA on our own document → FHIR pairs

Bottom line: fine-tuning a 2–4B VLM for document extraction is cheap, well-tooled and gives large measured gains. Two risks dominate: - Layout overfitting: models fine-tuned on templated synthetic data collapse on differently rendered documents. - Shipping: nobody has shown a LoRA adapter working with a vision projector in llama.cpp, so the low-risk path is to freeze the vision layers, or merge the adapter into the model and re-export it.

Free wins from schema design (see D3) come first and are much cheaper.

Measured gains, and the catch. - A Sept 2026 study fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B with LoRA (r16/α32, attention + MLP) on about 3K synthetic bank checks [15]. - In-distribution F1 rose to about 0.98: 3B went 0.847→0.983, beating GPT-5 zero-shot at 0.928. - On a differently rendered check set, the fine-tuned 3B fell to 0.593 [15]. - RAPTOR+ (June 2026) fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B, 4B and 8B on just 176 synthetic clinical forms [14]. - 2B reading accuracy rose from 62.3% to 88.3%. - Strict per-document safety reached only 21% at 2B and 26% at 4B, against 61% at 8B [14]. Fine-tuning narrows the size gap but does not close it. - A synthetic-to-real gap appears again in a care-transition study: about 97% on synthetic documents, about 85% on real scans [51] (medium confidence). - Implication: training data must mix real or realistically degraded scans across document families and the four languages, not just one rendered template.

A cheaper first step. Fix the prompt and schema first (see D3, where the VAREX evidence sits); extraction training adds on top [30].

Data. - No public dataset pairs medical document images with FHIR. Synthea is the only licence-clean source of FHIR ground truth: Apache-2.0, FHIR R4, covers all target resource types [52]. - Its demographics are US-only [52]. - There is no maintained European fork. A German study found only 7 of 16 Synthea resource types matched German core profiles without conversion [53]. - A realistic pipeline therefore looks like this: Synthea or our own FHIR → localised ES/DE/RO document templates → rendered and degraded images, plus a de-identified real set. That is our inference. - Reward functions for RL fine-tuning (GRPO) that score schema validity exist only as code patterns, with no published small-VLM results [54].

Tooling and cost (as of 2026-10-22). - TRL v1.16.0 does VLM supervised fine-tuning with PEFT LoRA [55]. - Unsloth covers Qwen3.5 0.8B–122B vision fine-tuning. It needs about 5 GB VRAM for bf16 LoRA at 2B and about 10 GB at 4B, and advises against 4-bit QLoRA for Qwen3.5 [56]. - LLaMA-Factory lists Qwen3-VL 2B/4B, Qwen3.5, Gemma 3/3n and MiniCPM-V 4.6 [57]. - MLX-VLM does LoRA and GRPO on Apple Silicon [58]. - Google ships official MedGemma LoRA and RL notebooks [59]. - Measured cost: 6–14 A100-80GB GPU-hours for 3B/7B on about 3K samples [15]. That is tens of dollars per run on rented GPUs, which is our arithmetic; no published price exists. The real cost is building the dataset, not compute.

D3. Structured, grammar-constrained JSON output

Bottom line: keep the intermediate-schema-then-map design, and make the grammar schema-specific instead of a generic "JSON array of objects". Do this through llamadart 0.15's responseFormat, with: - a flat schema: no $defs, required fields first, enums for closed value sets; - a "reason free, constrain late" step for the small model; - FHIR validation on the device after mapping.

Strict constraints guarantee form. They can cost accuracy at ≤3B, which is exactly our size class.

Why the schema matters most at our size. VAREX found that small VLMs fail mostly on format. Non-compliance jumps from 2–3% on flat schemas to 68–69% when the schema uses $defs. Just inlining $defs lifted Qwen3-VL-2B from 27.4% to 91.8% on that subset. Qwen3-VL-2B was non-compliant on 56% of image inputs, against 17% of text inputs [7]. Fix the prompt and schema before training; extraction training adds on top (D2).

What llama.cpp does today. - The built-in schema converter. It still handles only a subset of JSON Schema and silently skips what it can't handle: nested $ref, anyOf mixed with properties, number bounds, prefixItems. The schema also isn't injected into the prompt, so the prompt must still describe the structure [64]. - 2026 issue status (checked 2026-10-22): - Closed: large $ref/$defs schemas silently dropping the constraint (closed as completed, but no fix commit was identified, so re-test) [65], and the grammar not applying when the model's thinking mode is on [66]. - Not fixed: the deep-nesting crash (CVE-2026-52130), closed as "not planned" with its nesting-cap PR still open, and a minItems OOM, still open [67]. - New and still open: an optional property listed before a required one is silently dropped. The output is still valid JSON [68]. - llguidance is the stronger engine [69]. It errors on unsupported schemas instead of dropping them, keeps property order, and costs about 50 µs per token. It needs a Rust build flag.

What llamadart 0.15.6 changes for us. - Its own converter. It ships its own Dart port of the schema-to-grammar converter, so the upstream server crash bugs don't apply directly [2]. - Fails loudly. Schemas it can't represent (untyped properties, non-local $ref, too-deep nesting, cycles) throw instead of degrading silently, and grammars over the repetition limit are rejected before generation [2]. - Inherits the ordering rule. Its object rule uses the same required-first split, so the optional-before-required field-order bug above (llama.cpp #30321) probably applies. That is an inference from the code: test it, and put required fields first. - No llguidance [2]. - No constraints on the LiteRT-LM backend yet (open, P2), although upstream LiteRT-LM now exposes them [5].

Accuracy: constraints help form, can hurt content. - Small models gain. On 0.6–4B models, constraints took validity to 100% and lifted content accuracy, for example Llama-3.2-3B from 0.777 to 0.991. They fix type errors but not semantic ones [70]. At 8B, constrained decoding was neutral to slightly positive on accuracy [71]. - The "constraint tax." A May 2026 study found that at ≤3B, a hard answer-only schema gave 100% validity but cost 8.7 points of accuracy on average, up to 20 points for individual models and 43.5 on a tool-call task. Its fix is to "reason free, constrain late": delayed constraints reached 40.7% against 26.8% for answer-only [8]. - Independent confirmation. StructureBench (IJCAI 2026) tested 11 on-device models of 0.5–8B. It found constraints enforce structure but "may even degrade performance for smaller models" [9]. A separate "format tax" study puts most of the loss on format instructions rather than the decoding mask, and also recovers accuracy by separating reasoning from formatting [72]. So the direction is verified by independent groups; the exact sizes are not. - Draft-then-constrain. Draft freely, then decode constrained with the draft in context. It gains up to 24 points at 1–1.5B [73]. - Caveat. The constraint-tax and draft-then-constrain studies both use reasoning tasks, not document extraction, so the transfer is a hypothesis to test in D4's harness. - Already partly built. Our pipeline already has a free-text OCR path, which is a natural "draft" for the constrained pass. That is our inference.

FHIR-specific evidence. - CFG4FHIR (MIE 2026) builds grammars from FHIR R5 schemas with relaxed key order [74]. - It reaches 76.5% valid resources, against 41.2% with JSON-schema guidance and 17.6% unguided. - That is on only 17 cases with a 70B model. - Its repo has no licence, and enums can come out "valid but wrong" [75]. - Infherno rejected a full FHIR grammar as too costly [76]. - Taken together, this supports our current design: a small intermediate schema constrained by grammar, then a deterministic FHIR mapping. Not a full FHIR grammar on a 2B model. - On-device validation after mapping. fhir_r4_validation 0.14.0 (2026-10-16) checks structure, cardinality, bindings and FHIRPath invariants in Dart. Its models are R4B, not R4, so its fit for our R4 resources needs checking [10].

Other runtimes. - Apple Foundation Models now combines on-device image understanding with @Generable guided generation, and accepts Core AI-exported custom models [77]. Apple platforms only. - LiteRT-LM offers llguidance constraints (JSON Schema, Lark, regex) in C++ [78]. Reported bugs, status unverified: end-of-output allowed inside JSON strings, and no spaces from SentencePiece models [79].

D4. Benchmarking mapping accuracy

Bottom line: no public benchmark exists for turning document photos or scans into FHIR, in any language. The 2026 extraction benchmarks have, however, converged on a scoring design that can be reused almost as-is. So this is a build-our-own job on well-trodden methodology; Recommendation 3 assembles the harness specification.

Metrics that have converged in 2026. - Matching and F1. LlamaIndex's ExtractBench pairs predicted records to gold records one-to-one using the Hungarian algorithm (an optimal one-to-one assignment). Its matching cost is the number of mismatched subfields. It flattens the output to one cell per scalar and scores micro-F1 per document, then averages across documents [80]. - A missing key counts the same as an explicit null. Every scalar in the schema counts toward both precision and recall, so a correct null earns credit and an invented value costs precision [80]. - The evaluator is Apache-2.0 and actively maintained [81]. - Per-field comparators. Contextual AI's ExtractBench, a different benchmark with the same name, declares a comparator for each field in the schema: exact, case-insensitive, fuzzy, numeric tolerance, and an LLM judge only for free text [82]. - Omissions and hallucinations. The same benchmark gives every value one of three states, present, null or MISSING, which yields clean omission and hallucination rates [82]. - Invalid output. Invalid JSON stays in the denominator. In that benchmark it dominated failures: validity was 37–51% [82]. - FHIR validity rate. A FHIR-specific paper scores validity as the share of bundles the HL7 validator passes with no errors, and scores coding by exact code match. Its headline numbers are partly placeholders by the authors' own note, so only its method is usable [83]. - No FHIR-specific matching metric. No FHIR paper retrieved defines resource-level matching plus field-level F1. That part has to be designed in-house.

Adapting the metrics to FHIR. LlamaIndex ExtractBench's strict typing ("1,000" ≠ 1000) and its order-sensitive scalar lists would mis-score FHIR data. Three things need custom comparators: - valueQuantity needs a numeric comparison after unit conversion. NLM's ucum-lhc provides UCUM validation and conversion, but its custom licence needs review [84]. - Lists like coding[] and given[] need set semantics instead of order-sensitive comparison. - FHIR partial dates (YYYY or YYYY-MM) need a comparison that respects their precision.

Partial credit for codes based on the code hierarchy is published only for ICD [85]. Tiered credit for SNOMED, LOINC and RxNorm would be our own design. For example: exact code = 1, matching display text but no code = partial, same RxNorm ingredient = partial.

Datasets and harness tooling. - Closest template: IBM's VAREX (CVPRW 2026). - It generates 1,777 synthetic documents by filling templates, so ground truth is exact. Each document is offered as plain text, layout text, image, and layout text plus image [30]. - Its headline finding, that models below 4B return the schema instead of values ("schema echo", costing 45–65 points), drives D2 and D3 [30]. - VAREX's code is Apache-2.0. Its dataset licence is stated only as "permissive" [86]. - Text→FHIR work exists but doesn't transfer. - FHIR Workbench reports Note2FHIR F1 of only 0.04–0.36 [87]. - CIRCA uses a deterministic FHIR mapper but needs credentialed MIMIC access [88]. - flexpa/llm-fhir-eval shows a promptfoo harness with FHIRPath and validator assertions. It has no licence file, so reuse is not permitted by default, and it is barely maintained: copy the idea, not the code [89]. - Infherno is text-only and also unlicensed [90].

LLM-as-judge. A 2026 scoping review of 49 healthcare studies found 73.5% did no risk-of-bias testing and 26.5% used no expert validators. It warns that a judge which shares blind spots with the system it grades can make errors look like agreement [91]. Use deterministic scoring for every coded or factual field, and a human-validated judge only for free text (dosage text, notes).

Statistics. - Resample whole documents in a paired bootstrap. VAREX could not separate models within about 0.5 points even with 1,777 documents [30]. A clustered or hierarchical bootstrap handles fields nested in documents [92]. - No published "N documents to detect a 3–5 point F1 gap" exists for extraction. Card et al. recommend simulation-based power analysis [93]. The practical route is a pilot of 30–50 documents to estimate variance, then simulate the needed N. That is our inference, not a sourced figure.

Measuring on devices. - llama.cpp's llama-bench measures only text throughput: no image-encode time, time-to-first-token or RAM [94]. Those need custom instrumentation. - Thermal state must be controlled, because a few consecutive runs trigger CPU down-clocking [95]. - A 2026 Galaxy S25 Ultra study found only models up to about 3B gave usable interactivity under llama.cpp (medium confidence) [96].

Cross-dimension insights

  • The runtime upgrade unlocks every other recommendation. Schema-specific JSON (D3) needs llamadart's responseFormat with its loud failures. A downloadable LoRA (D2) needs its setLoraSource API. Qwen3.5 and NuExtract3 (D1a) are validated on the Qwen3.5 code path, which 0.6.8 predates. So the 0.6.8 → 0.15.x migration comes first. Its breaking changes and the untested Qwen3-VL/MedGemma support (D1b) are the first risks to retire.
  • Format failure ties model choice, grammar and fine-tuning together.
    • Small models lose most points on format [30].
    • Grammars fix form but can tax accuracy at ≤3B [8].
    • Extraction fine-tunes (NuExtract3, RAPTOR+) mostly fix format and localisation [13][14].
    • So the order is: schema design (free), then constrained decoding with a free-reasoning step, then an extraction-tuned base model, then our own LoRA. Measure each step on the D4 harness before moving to the next.
  • LiteRT-LM may be the answer on speed, but it opens the structured-output gap. It may be faster on phone GPUs (one unverified test, D1b), but it loses constrained JSON and multi-LoRA through llamadart (D2, D3). Re-evaluate it once llamadart issue #174 (LiteRT-LM constraints) lands, or if Gemma 4 or InternVL3.5 win on D4.
  • Licence shapes the tier strategy. MedGemma derivatives carry HAI-DEF obligations, including any model trained on MedGemma's outputs to perform like it [33]. An Apache-2.0 Qwen3.5 or NuExtract3 base avoids that for both shipping and LoRA.
  • The benchmark and the training data are the same asset. A VAREX-style synthetic pipeline (Synthea → localised templates → rendered and degraded images) gives exact ground truth for both D4 and D2 [30][52]. Hold out real de-identified scans for testing, so the 0.98 → 0.59 synthetic-to-real collapse can't hide [15].

Recommendations

In order of execution. Recommendations 1 and 3 can run in parallel; the schema change in 2 is measured on the harness from 3 before it ships.

  1. Upgrade llamadart from 0.6.8 to 0.15.x in a spike branch.
    • Retire these risks first: the breaking APIs from 0.11 and 0.14, the iOS 16.4 / macOS 14 minimum, and whether Qwen3-VL-2B and MedGemma 4B still load with their mmproj on all four targets.
    • Feeds: architecture spine (runtime decision), roadmap risk.
    • Confidence: high on the package facts [38]. Model compatibility is unverified.
  2. Replace the generic jsonArray GBNF with a schema-specific responseFormat.
    • Use one flat intermediate schema per pipeline phase: no $defs, required fields first, enums for closed sets.
    • Keep a free-text step before the constrained pass, either through the OCR text or a draft.
    • Then add a fhir_r4_validation pass after mapping, checking R4B vs R4 first.
    • Feeds: architecture spine (processing pipeline).
    • Confidence: high on mechanism [7][2]; medium on accuracy transfer, since the tax studies use reasoning tasks [8].
  3. Build the D4 benchmark before any model swap or training.
    • Synthetic EN/ES/DE/RO documents with exact ground truth, plus a de-identified real hold-out.
    • Scoring: ExtractBench-style Hungarian matching and per-document F1, using its Apache-2.0 evaluator plus FHIR comparators (UCUM, code sets, partial dates).
    • Also report validity rate, omissions and hallucinations, and a document-clustered bootstrap.
    • Measure image-encode time, time-to-first-token and peak RAM on a device matrix.
    • Feeds: brief (feasibility), roadmap estimates.
    • Confidence: high on method [80][30].
  4. Benchmark the model candidates on the D4 harness.
    • Advanced tier: NuExtract3 vs Qwen3.5-4B vs MedGemma 1.5 vs MedGemma 1 (current).
    • Lite tier: Qwen3.5-2B and NuExtract 2.0-2B against Qwen3-VL-2B (current).
    • Feeds: the AI model configuration.
    • Confidence: medium. The ranking rests on vendor numbers, and one was disputed [11].
  5. Treat LoRA as a later phase, gated on the benchmark.
    • If pursued: train an Apache-2.0 base with vision layers frozen, so the adapter is a small text-side GGUF loadable through setLoraSource.
    • Prove LoRA + mmproj end to end in a half-day experiment first.
    • Mix real scans with synthetic ones.
    • Feeds: roadmap risk.
    • Confidence: medium on gains [14][15]; low on shipping, since there is no end-to-end evidence [16].
  6. Do not adopt the Apple Foundation Models, ML Kit Prompt API or Cactus paths for extraction. Each fails a hard gate:
    • Apple is Apple-only, and its custom adapters are obsolete [17].
    • ML Kit is Android-only, beta and capped at 4K tokens [6].
    • Cactus has no Windows build, and its licence caps free use [49].
    • Feeds: architecture spine (runtime decision).
    • Confidence: high for Apple and Cactus, medium for ML Kit.

Decisions since publication

These were recorded on 2026-10-22 in _bmad-output/specs/spec-on-device-document-to-fhir-extraction/, which is now the contract for this work. The findings above are unchanged and remain dated evidence.

  • Gold-set size and code scoring (open question 7).
    • Start with a 40-document synthetic pilot (10 per language) to estimate variance; a simulation then sets N. Plan for about 100 synthetic documents per language until then.
    • Score codes by exact match, and report display-text match as a secondary metric. Partial credit is deferred.
  • The other six open questions are experiments, and the spec assigns each to a capability:
    • 1 and 5 to CAP-1 (runtime upgrade);
    • 2 to CAP-7 (LoRA + mmproj);
    • 3 to CAP-2 (schema-constrained output);
    • 4 to CAP-6 (model selection);
    • 6 to CAP-3 (FHIR validation).
  • Minimum OS. iOS 16.4 and macOS 14 are accepted for the llamadart upgrade. The current minimums are 16.0 and 12.0.
  • Real hold-out.
    • Only documents whose owner consents; today that is the developer's own.
    • Stored outside the repo, never sent to any service, never used for training.
    • Gold FHIR is labelled by hand.
    • Reported as its own stratum, never pooled with synthetic scores.
  • Synthetic set. The generator must emit the gold FHIR with each document. Documents made earlier by agents count only if their FHIR ground truth exists.
  • Ship rule for a model swap or LoRA. All four must hold on the harness:
    • the 95% paired bootstrap CI of the field-F1 gain excludes zero;
    • the gain is at least 3 points;
    • no language stratum drops by more than its CI;
    • it meets the performance budget.
  • Performance budget. Measure the current tier model on each device first. A candidate may be at most 25% slower per page, and must finish without running out of memory on the tier's lowest-RAM device.
  • Licences. The VAREX dataset is not used; we generate our own. ucum-lhc is used only in the harness and is never shipped in the app.
  • Still open:
    • EU MDR status, for regulatory review before the app makes any public claim about extraction accuracy.
    • Consent and de-identification, to define before training on real scans or adding anyone else's documents.
